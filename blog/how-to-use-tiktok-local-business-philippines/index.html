How to Use TikTok to Get Customers in Your Own City

By Daryl Urbanski Published September 2, 2026 · 8 min read

TikTok can genuinely bring you local customers, not just broad viral reach, if you deliberately signal location in your content and use it, rather than assuming the platform is only for national or global audiences. The mistake most local businesses make with TikTok is treating it as a lottery ticket for viral reach, when the more reliable use for a local business is far more targeted: showing up for people who are near enough to actually walk into your store or book your service.

I have watched local businesses dismiss TikTok entirely, assuming it only rewards national trends and dance content, while missing that the platform's discovery system is genuinely capable of surfacing content to a local audience when the content itself signals that it matters locally. This is exactly the angle we take with local businesses on the platform.

Why TikTok isn't just for going viral nationally

TikTok's recommendation system responds to signals in your content, your location tag, your captions, spoken location references, and it can and does surface local content to nearby viewers who are more likely to actually engage with something relevant to their own area. A national viral hit is not the only outcome worth aiming for. A steady stream of views from people who could genuinely become customers is a better outcome for most local businesses than a large, unconnected national audience.

Step 1: Actually say where you are, on camera and in text

This sounds obvious and is skipped constantly. Mention your city or area naturally within your video, in your caption, and use location-relevant hashtags alongside your product or service hashtags. This gives the platform's system a clear signal about who this content is genuinely relevant to.

Step 2: Show your actual physical presence

Content filmed genuinely inside your shop, at your actual location, with real customers or real staff, reads differently to both the algorithm and to viewers than generic content that could belong to any business anywhere. This is one of the few genuine advantages a small local business has over a large national brand on the platform: real, specific, unmistakably local presence.

Step 3: Lean into local relevance in your content ideas

Beyond simply mentioning your location, make content that is genuinely, specifically relevant to your area: a local event you are near, a local reference your community would recognize, a genuine local customer's story. This kind of specificity performs better with a local audience than generic content ever will, because it signals you are actually part of the community rather than broadcasting at it.

Step 4: Use TikTok Search behavior deliberately

A growing share of TikTok usage functions like a search engine, particularly among younger users, who increasingly search TikTok directly for recommendations, including local ones, the way an older generation might search Google. Content genuinely answering a specific local question, like "best [service] in [your city]," has a real chance of surfacing when someone searches exactly that phrase on the platform itself, the same instinct behind ranking in the Google local pack.

Step 5: Engage with your actual local comments

When someone local comments, respond in a way that reinforces your location and your business, rather than a generic thanks. A specific, local-flavored reply builds real community engagement and signals to anyone reading the comments that you are genuinely present and responsive in this specific area.

What kind of content works for this specifically

  • A quick tour or demonstration filmed at your actual location
  • A local customer's genuine story or reaction, with permission
  • Answering a locally-relevant question directly, in a way that would come up in a local search
  • A behind-the-scenes look that establishes you as a real, physical presence in the area, not an anonymous online seller

What doesn't need to be complicated here

You do not need to chase national trends, dance challenges, or viral audio that has nothing to do with your business, unless a genuine, natural fit exists. Trying to force relevance to something trending nationally, when it has nothing to do with your local business, usually reads as forced and rarely converts into an actual local customer walking through your door.

Measuring whether this is actually working for you

Views alone do not tell you whether TikTok is bringing you real local customers. Watch instead for: are new customers mentioning they found you on TikTok specifically, are your DMs and comments coming from people who reference your local area, and is your engagement coming from accounts that appear to genuinely be local rather than scattered nationally or internationally. These are better indicators of local traction than raw view count.

The honest limitation

TikTok, like any platform, takes real consistent effort to build traction on, and results are rarely immediate. Treat it the same way as the rest of your content strategy: a steady, consistent rhythm over months, not a single viral attempt you judge after a handful of posts, the same discipline covered in our guide to posting consistently.
