How to Start Facebook and TikTok Live Selling for Your Business

By Daryl Urbanski Published September 1, 2026 · 8 min read

You start live selling by getting the legal requirements sorted before your first stream, not after, since Philippine regulation now requires live sellers on any platform to register for a DTI Trustmark, then by setting up the platform-specific requirements, and finally by treating your first several streams as a learning process rather than expecting an immediate hit. Live selling works because it combines real-time demonstration, urgency, and trust in a way a static post cannot, but the regulatory side of it has changed recently in ways many sellers have not caught up to yet.

I have built and run direct-response selling systems for years, across formats from webinars to live broadcasts, and the mechanics of what makes a live sale close are remarkably consistent. What has changed here, and changed fast, is the compliance side, and that is worth getting right before you go live even once.

The regulatory requirement almost nobody has caught up to yet

Under the Internet Transactions Act, live sellers on any platform, TikTok, Facebook, Shopee, Instagram, are now required to hold a DTI Trustmark, also called the E-Commerce Philippine Trustmark. This applies regardless of your sales volume. A platform holding its own trustmark as a company, TikTok for instance, does not cover you as an individual seller. You need your own. This has genuinely caught a significant number of active live sellers off guard, since it is a change from how things worked previously, and it is exactly the kind of detail worth sorting out before you build a following around live selling rather than after.

Platform requirements, separately

TikTok Shop requires, at minimum, a TikTok account in good standing, and LIVE access, which is typically unlocked once you reach a follower threshold, commonly cited around 1,000 followers. Selling through TikTok Shop specifically also requires seller registration, with the requirements scaling up as your registration status moves from individual seller to a DTI-registered business name.

Facebook Live has a lower technical barrier to entry, since going live itself does not require the same follower threshold, but selling through it typically means directing viewers to message you or click through to complete a purchase manually, rather than the built-in checkout TikTok Shop provides.

What you actually need before your first stream

  • A stable phone, decent lighting, and reasonably clear audio. This matters more than expensive equipment. A well-lit, audible stream on a phone outperforms a poorly lit one on better gear.
  • Your products physically ready and organized, so you are not fumbling during the stream to find what you are describing
  • A clear, simple way for viewers to actually complete a purchase, whether that is TikTok Shop's built-in checkout or a clear instruction to message you
  • Your registration and compliance requirements sorted, as covered above

Why live selling works, mechanically

Three things happen simultaneously that a static post cannot replicate:

Real demonstration. Viewers see the actual product, in real hands, not a curated photo. This addresses the trust hesitation that stops a lot of online purchases before they start.

Real-time urgency. Limited stock genuinely moving in front of viewers, "three left," creates a kind of urgency that a static "limited stock" caption cannot match, because it is happening visibly, not just claimed.

Real-time questions answered. A viewer's specific hesitation gets addressed immediately, on stream, rather than sitting unanswered in a comment for hours.

Running your first few streams

Treat the first several as learning sessions, not proof of concept. Watch specifically for:

  • What times your actual audience shows up, which is often different from when you assumed
  • Which products generate real questions and engagement versus which ones sit quietly
  • How viewers actually phrase their purchase intent, so you know exactly what to listen for

Do not judge live selling as a channel based on one or two early streams. The skill of running an engaging, converting live stream develops with repetition, in a way that is genuinely different from writing a good static post.

What to say, structurally

A workable simple structure for each stream:

  1. Open with something that stops a scroller, not a slow warm-up. The first few seconds decide whether someone stays.
  2. Show, don't just describe. Hold the product, demonstrate it being used, let people see texture, size, and detail a photo cannot convey.
  3. Answer questions as they come in, by name where possible, since being personally addressed keeps viewers engaged and watching for their own name to come up.
  4. Create genuine urgency around what's actually true, limited stock, a real time-limited offer, not a manufactured one.
  5. Give an unmistakably clear way to buy, repeated periodically throughout, since viewers join mid-stream constantly and miss instructions given only once at the start.

The honest limitation

Live selling takes real time, genuinely more per sale in the moment than a static post does, and it takes practice to get comfortable and effective on camera. It is not a replacement for the rest of your content strategy. It is an additional, high-conversion channel worth adding once your compliance and basic content rhythm are already in place, not the first thing to attempt before those foundations exist.
