How to Stop Answering the Same Customer Question Twenty Times a Day

You answer it once, properly, then let saved replies and FAQ automation handle every repeat. Find your five most-asked questions, usually price, hours, location, delivery, and availability, then put those answers into Messenger's FAQ automation, your Google Business Profile, and your page itself, so customers get the answer instantly without you typing. You step in only for questions that genuinely need you.

There is a particular kind of tiredness that comes from typing "9am to 6pm po, Monday to Saturday" for the eleventh time before lunch. It is not difficult. It is just an endless, low-grade drain on attention you need for the parts of the business only you can do. Removing that drain is exactly what our AI & automation work targets.

Step 1: Find out what you're actually repeating

Do not guess. Guessing produces a list of what you think customers ask, which is usually wrong in useful ways. Open your Messenger inbox and read the last fifty conversations. For each one, note the first question the customer asked. Tally them. Most businesses find that four or five questions account for the large majority of first messages, and often one or two of them are things the owner never thought to advertise. This exercise takes about thirty minutes and it is the difference between building the right FAQ and building a generic one.

Step 2: Write each answer once, properly

For each of your top questions, write the answer you would give on your best day. Not the shortest possible version, the clearest one. A good answer answers the actual question directly first, adds the one thing they would ask next, and gives a clear next step. For example, instead of "₱500 po," try "₱500 po for the basic service, around 45 minutes. If you want to book, here's the link: [link]" That single addition removes an entire follow-up exchange.

Step 3: Put the answers into Messenger FAQ automation

Meta Business Suite includes an FAQ feature where you set up common questions and pre-written answers, which customers can tap to get instantly. This is the most useful of Meta's native automation tools for a business with repetitive questions, because unlike a plain instant reply it actually resolves the question rather than just acknowledging it. You can attach buttons linking to your booking page or website. Setup lives in Meta Business Suite under Inbox then Automations, alongside your instant replies. It is free.

Step 4: Set up saved replies for your staff

FAQ automation handles the customer tapping a question. Saved replies handle the situations where a person is responding but should not have to retype a standard answer. The difference matters if you have staff. Saved replies also solve a second problem: consistency. When three different people answer the same question three slightly different ways, customers notice, and the differences occasionally become actual problems, like one person quoting a price another does not honor. When a reply does need a personal touch, our guide to writing replies with AI in your own voice helps.

Step 5: Put the answers where people look before messaging

A meaningful share of repeated questions exist because the answer was not findable anywhere else. Fix the source:

  • Google Business Profile. Hours, services, and description filled in completely. Many customers check here first and never message at all if they find what they need. Our guide to setting up your Google Business Profile covers this.
  • Your Facebook page's About section. Kept current, not written once in 2021.
  • A visible FAQ on your website, if you have one.
  • Your pinned post, for things that change seasonally, like holiday hours.

Every question answered here is a message that never arrives.

Step 6: Review the list every couple of months

Your top questions shift. A new service, a price change, a new location, or a holiday season all generate new repeated questions. Rereading your recent inbox every couple of months keeps the automation aimed at what people are actually asking now rather than what they asked last year.

The questions you should never automate

Some questions look repetitive but are not:

  • Anything about a complaint or something going wrong
  • Anything where the answer depends on specifics you would need to check
  • Anything involving money coming back to the customer
  • Anything medical, legal, or safety-related

An automated answer to a complaint reads as dismissive, even when the information is correct. Route these to a person, quickly.

What good looks like

After a few weeks, the shape of your inbox should change. Fewer messages overall, because more people found the answer before messaging. And the messages that do arrive should be more substantive, the ones that genuinely need judgment, which are also the ones more likely to turn into a sale. If your volume has not dropped at all, the usual cause is that the answers are automated but not discoverable, meaning they exist inside Messenger but not on your Google profile or page where people look first.

When repetition is a symptom of something else

If you have staff and you are still personally answering the same questions constantly, the problem may not be the questions. It may be that your team does not have clear, agreed answers they are confident giving, so everything escalates to you by default. That is a different problem, and no amount of Messenger automation solves it, because the bottleneck is not the software. That pattern, where everything routes through the owner because nobody else is sure what they are allowed to say, is one of the most common reasons an owner-operated business cannot grow past the owner's own capacity.
