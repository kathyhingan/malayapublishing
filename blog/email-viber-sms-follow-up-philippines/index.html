Email, Viber, or SMS: How to Follow Up With Filipino Customers

By Daryl Urbanski Published August 1, 2026 · 8 min read

For most Filipino small businesses, Viber and Messenger outperform email for follow-up, because that is where your customers actually spend their attention, while SMS remains the most reliable channel when a message absolutely must be seen, since it needs no app or data connection. Email works well for a narrower slice of customers, mainly office-based professionals, and it works poorly as a first-choice channel for local, walk-in, and Messenger-native businesses. The right answer is rarely one channel. It is knowing which channel earns which message.

I have run follow-up sequences across every channel available, for clients from Manila to markets that live entirely on email. The single biggest mistake I see is a business defaulting to whichever channel is easiest for them to set up, rather than the one their actual customer checks. Getting this right matters most for online and e-commerce sellers and local businesses alike.

Start with where your customer already is, not where automation is easiest

Email is the easiest channel to automate cheaply, which is exactly why so many small business guides default to recommending it. But easy to automate and effective are different questions. A beautifully automated email sequence that nobody opens is worse than a manual Viber message that gets read in ten minutes. Before choosing a channel, answer honestly: where does this specific customer actually check messages during their day? For a corporate professional booking a consultation, that might genuinely be email. For most local, walk-in, and community-based businesses here, it is Messenger and Viber, by a wide margin.

Email: good for a narrower job than people assume

Email's real strength is longer-form, less urgent communication: a detailed proposal, a newsletter, a receipt with full details attached. It works well for professional services with a corporate client base who genuinely operate on email daily. Its weakness for most Philippine small businesses is simple: for a large share of customers, especially outside office-based work, email gets checked rarely, if at all. An automated reminder or follow-up sitting unread in an inbox nobody opens accomplishes nothing, regardless of how well it was written.

Messenger: the default for most local businesses

If your customers already found you through Facebook, message you to ask questions, and book through the same thread, Messenger is not a follow-up channel you are choosing. It is the channel you are already in. The constraint worth knowing, covered fully in our automation cluster: Meta restricts automated messages sent more than 24 hours after a customer's last message to you. This means Messenger follow-up beyond that window needs to come from a real person, sent manually, rather than an automated sequence. That is a genuine limitation, and it is also usually fine, because a manual message from an actual person often converts better than an automated one anyway.

Viber: strong for relationship-based follow-up

Viber holds a distinct place here, particularly for maintaining relationships with existing and repeat customers through broadcast-style updates, since it does not carry the same 24-hour restriction that Messenger does for a subscribed broadcast list. The honest limitation, covered in our automation article: true automated Viber messaging through a chatbot carries a real monthly cost, so for most small businesses the practical version is manual, saved-reply-based communication, not a fully automated sequence.

SMS: the fallback that actually reaches everyone

SMS has one property none of the others fully share: it needs no app, no data connection, and no platform account to receive. For a message that absolutely must land, an appointment reminder, a delivery update, a time-sensitive offer, SMS remains the most dependable option. The honest tradeoff is cost. Sending SMS through a bulk gateway costs a small amount per message. For low volume, that cost is trivial against what a missed appointment or a lost sale costs you. At high volume, it is worth pricing properly rather than assuming. Our guide to appointment reminders covers the tradeoffs.

A practical way to decide, per message

Rather than picking one channel for your whole business, decide per message type:

  • Time-sensitive and must be seen: SMS
  • Ongoing conversation, active inquiry: Messenger
  • Relationship maintenance with existing customers, broadcast-style: Viber
  • Detailed, formal, or professional-service context: Email

Most small businesses end up using two channels well rather than one channel for everything, and that is the right outcome, not a sign of an unfinished strategy.

What actually determines success, regardless of channel

In my experience, the channel matters less than most people assume once you have picked the right one for the message. What separates a follow-up that converts from one that gets ignored:

Specificity. Referencing what the person actually asked about, not a generic template. Timing. A follow-up sent while interest is still warm outperforms the same message sent a week later on every channel I have tested. A genuinely small next step. Not "let us know if you're interested," but a specific, easy action.

I have watched businesses agonize over which platform to use while sending genuinely weak, generic messages on whichever platform they picked. Fix the message first. The channel amplifies what you send. It does not fix it.

Testing this for your own business

If you are unsure which channel actually works for your customers, this is measurable rather than a matter of opinion. Track, for a month, which channel's follow-ups actually get a response, separated by message type. The answer is often different from what an owner assumes, because assumptions here are usually based on which channel the owner personally prefers, not which one the customer actually uses.

The mistake that costs the most

Choosing a channel because it was easiest to automate, then blaming the follow-up strategy when it underperforms. The strategy was rarely the problem. The channel selection was.
