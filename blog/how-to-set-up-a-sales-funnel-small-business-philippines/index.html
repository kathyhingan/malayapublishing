How to Set Up a Simple Sales Funnel for a Filipino Small Business

A sales funnel is just the path a stranger takes from noticing you exist to becoming a paying customer, and for most small businesses here it has three stages: something that gets attention, a conversation that builds trust, and a clear ask that closes the sale. You do not need software or a complicated diagram to have one. You need those three stages to actually exist and connect, instead of ending at whichever one you happen to be comfortable with.

Early in my career, I built a single marketing funnel for a client, Neurogym, that generated over $1.6 million in under eight months, and grew to $7.5 million over three years. The mechanics behind that were not exotic. It was the same three stages done properly, at scale, with nothing skipped. The scale is different for a Filipino small business. The structure is not. This is the backbone of our lead generation work.

Why "just posting" isn't a funnel

Posting content is only the first stage. A lot of small businesses do that stage reasonably well and then stop, expecting sales to happen automatically once someone has seen a few posts. They rarely do, because nothing in the process ever asks for a decision. A funnel without a close is just entertainment with a business page attached.

Stage 1: Get attention

This is where most of the marketing advice you have already heard applies: your Google Business Profile, your social posts, word of mouth, referrals, and any paid ads you run. The job of this stage is narrow: make a stranger aware you exist and give them a reason to look closer. Do not try to sell everything at this stage. Its only job is earning the next step, which is usually a message, a follow, or a visit to your page.

Stage 2: Build trust through conversation

Someone noticed you. Now what? For most Filipino small businesses, this stage happens in Messenger or Viber, and it is where the real decision gets made. This is exactly the structure covered in our article on turning Messenger inquiries into customers: answer their real question, understand their real need, and present the right specific option. This stage is also where a lot of funnels leak. A slow reply, a vague answer, or a conversation that trails off without direction all lose someone who was already interested enough to reach out. If your instant reply and follow-up systems are not solid, this is where the funnel actually breaks, even though the attention stage looked fine.

Stage 3: Ask clearly for the sale

Covered in depth elsewhere, but the summary matters here: after the conversation has built enough trust and clarity, ask directly for the booking, the order, or the next concrete step. A funnel that never asks is not a funnel. It is a very polite dead end.

Mapping your current process onto these three stages

Take an honest look at how a customer actually moves through your current setup:

  1. Where do people first hear about you?
  2. What happens right after that? Do they message you, or does the trail go cold?
  3. Once they message, does the conversation reliably lead somewhere, or does it wander?
  4. Is there a clear moment where you ask for the sale, every time, or does it depend on who happens to be answering?

Most owners find a genuine gap in this exercise, and it is rarely the first stage. Attention is usually fine. The middle, the conversation that should build trust and lead somewhere, is where most funnels are actually broken.

A simple funnel you can build this week

Attention: One clear, specific offer posted consistently, plus your Google Business Profile fully filled in.

Conversation: Your Messenger instant reply live, answering the top question immediately, with a booking link included.

Close: A simple structure for every inquiry, following the answer, qualify, recommend, ask pattern from our Messenger conversion article.

That is a complete, working funnel. It does not require software, a landing page, or an email list to start. It requires the three stages to genuinely connect to each other.

Where a funnel breaks, and how to tell

Plenty of attention, few messages: the offer is unclear, or what you are attracting is not actually matched to what you sell. Revisit what you are posting and whether it invites a specific next step.

Plenty of messages, few sales: the conversation structure is the problem. This is the most common gap, and it is directly fixable with the conversion structure covered separately.

Sales happen but rarely repeat: this is a different funnel entirely, the one that turns a customer into a repeat customer. Do not solve this by pouring more into stage one. It will not fix a leak further down.

The mistake I see constantly, at every scale

Businesses obsess over the attention stage because it is the most visible and the most fun. More followers, more reach, a bigger campaign. Meanwhile the conversation and close stages, which is where the money actually changes hands, get almost no deliberate attention at all. The $1.6 million NeuroGym result did not come from a clever attention-getting trick. It came from a conversation and close process that was tested and refined relentlessly, sitting behind an attention stage that was honestly fairly ordinary. Fix your middle before you spend more on your top.

Building this without a big budget

A funnel is a sequence, not a purchase. You can build all three stages using free tools already covered across this cluster and our automation cluster: your existing social pages, Messenger's native automation, and a simple conversation structure you commit to using consistently. The businesses that succeed here are not the ones who spent the most. They are the ones whose three stages actually connect.
