How to Get More Google Reviews From Filipino Customers Using Viber, Messenger, and GCash Receipts

You get more Google reviews by making it as easy as possible for a customer to leave one at the exact moment they are already happy with you, using a direct review link sent through the channel they already use, usually Viber or Messenger, right after payment. The businesses that struggle with reviews are almost never dealing with unhappy customers. They are dealing with happy customers who simply never got asked at the right moment, in the right place.

If you have five-star service and three reviews to show for it, that gap is not a reflection of your business. It is a reflection of a process nobody ever built. Building that process is part of how we help local businesses get found and chosen.

Why reviews matter more here than almost anywhere else

Before choosing a business, most Filipino customers check reviews, ratings, and photos first. Reviews do two jobs at once: they influence whether Google shows you in the local pack at all, and they influence whether a stranger who does find you actually decides to message you instead of the business next to you. A profile with ten recent five-star reviews reads as trustworthy on sight. A profile with two reviews from three years ago reads as a question mark. Reviews are one of the biggest levers in ranking in the local pack.

Step 1: Get your direct review link

Before asking anyone for anything, get the direct link that takes a customer straight to your review box, no searching required. Open your Google Business Profile dashboard and look for "Ask for reviews," which will generate a shareable link you can send through any channel. If you have not created your profile yet, start with our guide to setting up your Google Business Profile.

Turn that link into a QR code as well, using a free QR generator, and keep both versions ready: the link for messaging, the QR code for anything printed or displayed in person.

Step 2: Ask at the exact right moment

The single biggest lever is timing, not wording. Ask right when a customer's satisfaction is at its highest, which is almost always right after the service is done or the product is delivered, not days later when the feeling has faded and the message from you feels like an afterthought.

Step 3: Ask through Messenger

If your customer already messaged you to book or order, and most walk-in and service businesses here run largely through Messenger, the follow-up review request belongs in that same thread. Something simple and personal works far better than a generic template:

"Thank you po for coming in today! If you have a minute, we'd really appreciate a quick Google review, it genuinely helps small businesses like ours. [link]"

Sending this as a saved reply means it costs you nothing extra to send every single time, and consistency is what turns this into a real system instead of something you remember to do occasionally. If your inbox is overwhelming, our AI & automation systems can send this follow-up for you automatically after every conversation.

Step 4: Ask through Viber

Viber does not have the same built-in messaging automation that Messenger offers, so this usually means a saved quick reply on your side, sent manually right after a job or delivery. If you run a Viber broadcast list for regular customers, this is also a natural fit for that channel, kept occasional and genuine rather than constant.

Step 5: Use the GCash payment moment

For businesses accepting GCash, the moment a customer confirms payment is a second natural ask point, separate from the moment of service. If you are sending an automatic payment confirmation or thank-you, that same message is a natural home for the review link. Two clean touches, one after the service and one after payment, tend to convert better than repeating the same ask twice.

Step 6: Put a QR code where people are already standing still

A QR code linked to your review page works well anywhere a customer has a spare thirty seconds and nothing else to look at: your counter, a table tent, the back of a receipt, a sticker near the door on the way out. Print quality does not need to be fancy. It needs to be visible and it needs to actually work when scanned, so test it yourself before putting it up.

What to actually say

Keep the ask short, specific, and honest. Avoid anything that sounds scripted or corporate. A few things that tend to work:

  • Thank them for something specific, not generic, if you can. "Thank you for trusting us with your daughter's birthday cake" beats "thank you for your business."
  • Make the ask feel optional, not required. Pressure shows, and it can backfire.
  • If you genuinely want a particular happy customer's review and they have not left one, a short personal follow-up a week or two later is reasonable. Repeated reminders are not.

What not to do

  • Do not offer a discount or incentive in exchange for a review. This violates Google's policies and can get reviews removed or your profile penalized.
  • Do not ask only your happiest customers while quietly skipping everyone else. Google can detect patterns that look filtered or gamed, and it damages trust with real customers who notice too.
  • Do not buy reviews. Beyond being against the rules, fake reviews are usually obvious to anyone reading them closely, and they cost you more credibility than having fewer real ones.

Responding to every review, not just the good ones

Reviews are not a one-way street. Responding shows customers, including future ones reading your profile, that someone is actually paying attention. A short, genuine thank-you on a positive review takes seconds. A calm, non-defensive response to a critical one matters even more, and we cover exactly how to do that in our guide to handling a bad Google review.

What we would build first, in your position

If you are starting from close to zero, we would not chase quantity first. We would fix the ask process, make it something that happens automatically after every single transaction, then let the number climb steadily instead of trying to force a burst of reviews all at once, which can actually look suspicious to Google if it happens too fast.
