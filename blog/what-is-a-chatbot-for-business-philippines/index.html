What Is a Chatbot, and Does Your Small Business Actually Need One?

By Kathy Urbanski Published July 27, 2026 · 9 min read

A chatbot is an automated assistant in your Messenger, Viber, or website that answers common customer questions and can collect details before a person takes over. You need one when the same questions flood your inbox faster than you can answer them and you are losing leads to slow replies. You do not need one if you get a handful of messages a day that you can answer well yourself, in which case free auto-replies and saved templates do the same job for nothing.

There is a lot of pressure right now to have AI in your business, and a lot of people selling it. Some of that pressure is warranted. Much of it is aimed at businesses that would get more value from fixing their reply templates. We take an honest line on this in our AI & automation work: build it only where it earns its keep.

What a chatbot actually does

Strip away the marketing and a chatbot does four things:

  1. Answers repeated questions without you typing the answer again
  2. Sorts inquiries so the ones needing a human get to you faster
  3. Collects information upfront, like the service they want or their preferred date
  4. Holds the conversation when you are asleep, busy, or with another customer

That is it. It is a filter and a first responder, not a salesperson.

The two kinds, and why the difference matters

Rule-based chatbots follow a scripted decision tree. The customer picks from options, or types a keyword, and the bot follows the branch. They are predictable and cheap. They break when someone asks something the script did not anticipate, which happens more than you would expect with real customers who do not know they are supposed to stay on script.

AI chatbots understand natural language and can handle unexpected phrasing without collapsing. They can respond in English, Filipino, or Taglish, and manage multi-step conversations. They cost more, though the price gap between the two has narrowed considerably.

The honest guidance: use structured, rule-based automation for known, predictable tasks like pricing, availability, hours, and booking. Use AI where the range of possible questions is genuinely wide and the cost of an occasional wrong answer is low.

The line you should not cross with either kind

Hand off to a human early for anything involving billing disputes, refunds, account changes, medical questions, legal questions, or anything that could cause real harm to a customer if answered wrong. The best automated systems are not the ones that answer everything. They are the ones that know where their boundary is and hand over cleanly. This matters commercially too. A bot that confidently gives a wrong answer about a medical service or a refund policy costs you more than the time it saved.

Five signs you actually need one

  1. You are answering the same five questions more than a dozen times a day
  2. You are losing leads specifically because replies come too slowly, not because of price or service
  3. Messages arrive across multiple channels and things fall through the cracks between them
  4. You have staff, and inquiries get inconsistent answers depending on who replies
  5. Your inbox volume has grown past what one person can hold in their head

Five signs you do not

  1. You get a handful of messages a day and handle them fine
  2. Your questions vary too much to script usefully
  3. Your customers specifically value talking to you personally, which is a real competitive advantage in some businesses and worth protecting
  4. You have not yet turned on the free auto-replies and FAQ answers that Meta already gives you
  5. Your actual problem is that nobody is watching the inbox, which a bot does not fix

That fourth one deserves emphasis. A meaningful number of businesses quoted for a chatbot would have solved most of their problem with the free native tools they already have and never enabled.

What it costs in the Philippines

Chatbot pricing here varies enormously depending on scope, and quotes differing wildly between providers is normal rather than a sign someone is overcharging. The main cost drivers:

  • Number of channels. A Messenger-only bot costs less than one running across Messenger, Viber, and your website simultaneously, since each channel needs its own design, testing, and ongoing support.
  • Integration complexity. A bot that answers canned questions is cheap. One that connects to your CRM, pulls customer history, and syncs with billing costs considerably more, because that integration work is genuinely complex.
  • Conversation complexity. Qualifying a prospect across property type, budget, location, and timeline needs far more design work than answering three FAQs.

The pattern worth understanding: a quote covers setup, meaning design, training, and integration, plus ongoing monthly maintenance. Some vendors front-load setup and charge less monthly. Others do the reverse. Both are legitimate. Compare the total over a year, not the headline number.

The mistake that wastes the money

The most common way a chatbot investment goes to waste is not connecting it to anything. A bot that captures leads but does not sync them into your CRM or book directly into your calendar just creates a new pile of manual work moving information from one place to another. If you are paying for automation and still copying details by hand afterward, you have bought a more expensive version of the problem.

Will it annoy your customers?

It can, if it is the only thing they can reach. Customers tolerate and often appreciate a bot that answers fast and hands over quickly. They resent one that traps them in a loop with no way to reach a person. Always give an obvious escape route to a human.

Where to start if you're not sure

Start free. Turn on Messenger's native instant replies and FAQ answers, which cost nothing, and run them for a month. Watch what still lands in your inbox that the automation could not handle. That list is your actual requirement, written by your real customers, and it is worth far more than a guess when you do go shopping for something paid.
