A One-Week Marketing Plan for a Business With No Marketing Team

By Daryl Urbanski Published August 10, 2026 · 8 min read

A week is enough time to put the fundamentals in place: know who you're actually selling to, fix how you present that on your page, set up one way to capture repeat customers, and run one small, deliberate ad test. It is not enough time to build a complete marketing engine, but it is enough to move from reactive, whenever-I-remember marketing to something with an actual shape, and that shape is what compounds over the following months.

I have built marketing systems at every scale, from solo operators to seven-figure campaigns, and the fundamentals barely change with size. What changes is how much time and money you have to execute them. This is the version built for someone with neither a marketing team nor a marketing budget beyond what they can personally spend a week on, and it is exactly how we start with many local businesses.

Before you start: what you'll need

  • Access to your Facebook and Instagram pages
  • A list of your past customers if you have one, even an incomplete one
  • A small test budget for Facebook ads, even a modest amount is enough to start learning
  • Your phone, since most of this happens on it

Monday: know exactly who you're selling to

Spend an hour writing down, specifically, who your best customers actually are. Not "everyone who needs this," but real detail: what they were dealing with right before they found you, what almost stopped them from buying, and what they actually said afterward. If you have past customers, this is detective work more than guessing, covered in depth in our keyword research article in the SEO cluster, and the same principle applies here to your marketing language, not just your search terms.

Tuesday: fix your page and profile

With that clarity, review your Facebook page, Instagram, and Google Business Profile. Does the description speak to that specific customer's actual situation, or does it describe your business the way you'd describe it to another business owner? Rewrite your bio and pinned content to speak directly to the result that customer wants, following the caption structure covered elsewhere in this cluster: the result first, one hesitation addressed, one clear next step.

Wednesday: build your capture system

Set up one simple way to start collecting your own customer list, as covered in our list-building article. This does not need to be sophisticated. A shared spreadsheet with a habit of adding every new contact, starting today, is enough to begin. If you already have past customer contacts scattered across old messages, spend today's remaining time consolidating what you can find into that one place.

Thursday: set up one real ad test

Using what you learned Monday, run one small, focused Facebook ad through Ads Manager rather than the Boost button, following the setup covered in our first ad article. One objective, one audience, one piece of creative that speaks to the specific customer you described on Monday. Do not judge it today. The point of Thursday is that it is running and learning, not that it has already produced results.

Friday: build your follow-up habit

Set up the fifteen-minute daily habit covered in our follow-up systems article: a simple way to see who needs contacting today, and a fixed time you commit to doing it. This is the piece that turns Monday through Thursday's work into compounding value rather than a one-time push.

The weekend: reflect and set the next week's priority

Look honestly at what actually got finished versus what you intended. Most people finish three of the five days properly in their first attempt, and that is a completely reasonable outcome. Note which piece needs another pass next week, and start there rather than trying to redo everything at once.

What you will not finish in a week, and that's fine

A genuinely optimized ad campaign, a large customer list, and a proven, repeatable retention system all take months of consistent effort, not a week. This plan is not meant to finish that work. It is meant to establish the shape of it, so that the effort you put in during the following weeks has an actual structure to build on rather than starting from zero each time.

Where most people stop, and how to avoid it

The most common failure is finishing the setup week and then letting the daily and weekly habits quietly lapse once the initial energy fades. The plan does not fail because any single step was wrong. It fails because Friday's habit, the one unglamorous piece that has to repeat every single day, stops happening. If you do only one thing consistently after this week, make it that one. Everything else in this plan produces value only in combination with a genuine, ongoing follow-up habit.

What comes after this week

Once the fundamentals from this week are running, the ongoing work becomes iterative rather than foundational: refining your ad based on what you learned, growing your list steadily, and using the retention strategies covered elsewhere in this cluster to get more value from the customers you already have. None of it requires a marketing team. It requires the habits this week was built to install.
