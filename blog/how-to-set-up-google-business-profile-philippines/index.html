Setting up your Google Business Profile means creating a free listing at business.google.com, choosing the right category, then verifying it, usually by a short video walkthrough of your storefront. Once verified, your business can show up on Google Maps and in local search results when someone nearby searches for what you sell. The whole thing takes about twenty minutes to start, though verification can take a few days.

If you have ever searched your own business name on Google and felt that small drop in your stomach when nothing came up, or worse, when a wrong number and an old address came up instead, you already know why this matters. You built something real. Google just does not know it exists yet.

That is not a small problem. It is the difference between a customer finding you tonight or finding your competitor instead, because their profile happened to get set up first. Getting found on Google is the foundation of our SEO & AI visibility work, and it starts here.

What is a Google Business Profile, and do you actually need one?

A Google Business Profile, or GBP, is the free listing that shows your business on Google Maps and in the map results (the "local pack") when someone searches nearby. If you sell to people who search for a business like yours near their location, phrases like "salon near me" or "aircon repair Calamba," you need one. It costs nothing and it is often the single highest-return thing a small business can do online, because it puts you in front of someone who is actively looking to buy, right now. This is exactly the problem we solve for local businesses across the Philippines.

Step 1: Check if a profile already exists

Before creating a new listing, search your business name plus your city on Google. Sometimes a profile already exists, created automatically by Google or added by a past customer, and it is sitting there unclaimed. If you see a business panel with an "Own this business?" link, claim that one instead of creating a duplicate. Two competing profiles for the same business confuses Google and can actually hurt your ranking, since Google is not sure which one is real.

Step 2: Create your listing at business.google.com

If nothing exists, go to business.google.com and sign in with a Google account. Use one your business will keep long-term, not a personal account tied to one employee, since losing access to that account later means losing control of your listing.

You will be asked for:

  • Business name. Use your real, legal business name exactly as it appears on your signage. Do not add keywords like "best" or your service type into the name field. Google's guidelines treat that as a violation, and it can get your listing suspended.
  • Category. This is one of the most important fields on the whole profile, because it decides which searches you show up for. Choose the most specific category available. "Dental clinic" beats "medical clinic" if that is genuinely what you are.
  • Location. If customers come to you, add your exact address. If you serve customers at their location, like a home cleaning or repair service, you can hide your address and set a service area instead.
  • Contact details. Phone number and website, if you have one.

Step 3: Verify your business

Verification is Google confirming that you are the real, legitimate operator of this business, not someone claiming a listing that is not theirs. Until you are verified, changes you make will not go live and your profile will not show up properly in search.

In the Philippines in 2026, video verification is the method you will most likely be offered. Google walks you through recording a short, continuous video, live inside the verification flow, not a pre-recorded clip. You will typically need to show:

  • Your signage with the business name clearly visible
  • The exterior of your location
  • The interior, and something that proves you are actually operating there, like your equipment, your stock, or your point of sale

Keep it to one continuous take. Walk from the signage to the interior without cutting the recording. The most common reason a video gets rejected is that the business name on the signage is never clearly shown. Avoid capturing faces or sensitive documents while filming.

Postcard verification, where Google mails a code to your address, still exists in some cases but has become less reliable and less common, especially for service-area businesses without a fixed storefront. If it is offered to you, take it only if video is not an option, since postal delivery timing in the Philippines is not something you want to depend on for a launch date.

Video verification review can take a few business days, not instant. Plan your launch timeline around that.

Filling out the rest of the profile properly

Verification gets you live. It does not get you ranking. Once verified, fill in every section Google gives you:

  • Business hours, including holiday hours. An outdated "closed" during your actual open hours is one of the fastest ways to lose a walk-in customer.
  • Photos. Add your logo, a cover photo, at least a few interior and exterior shots, and photos of your products or service in action. Real photos outperform stock photos, and businesses with photos consistently get more clicks than those without.
  • Business description, up to 750 characters. Describe what you actually do, in plain language, using the words a customer would search.
  • Services or products list, if your category supports it.
  • Messaging, if you want to accept direct messages through your profile. Turn this on only if someone is actually watching it, since an unanswered message on your own Google listing looks worse than no messaging option at all.

Common mistakes that get a profile suspended or buried

  • Stuffing keywords into the business name field. Google can suspend a listing for this.
  • Choosing the wrong category "for the traffic," rather than what the business truly is.
  • Multiple business owners creating duplicate listings for the same location under slightly different names.
  • Leaving the profile untouched after the initial setup. Google favors profiles that stay active, with regular photo updates, responses to reviews, and posts.
  • Changing your name, address, or category right after verification. Major edits from a newly verified account can trigger a fresh round of verification.

What happens after you are live

A verified, complete profile makes you eligible to appear in the local pack, the map view most people check first before they check anything else. From there, two things move you further up: reviews, which we cover in a companion article, and consistency, meaning your name, address, and phone number match exactly across every other place your business is listed online. We break down the full ranking picture in our guide to ranking in the Google local pack.

Setting this up correctly once is worth more than tweaking it constantly afterward. Get the foundation right, keep it updated, and let it work quietly in the background while you run the rest of your business.
