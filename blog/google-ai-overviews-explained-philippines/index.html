AI Overviews Explained: Will It Take My Google Traffic, and What Should You Actually Do About It

An AI Overview is the summarized answer Google sometimes shows at the top of search results, generated by AI, that pulls information from several web pages and links out to them as sources. It does not replace the regular results below it, and getting cited in one requires the same fundamentals as ranking well in normal search: a page that is genuinely helpful, clearly written, and already trusted enough to rank. There is no separate trick for it.

If you have seen headlines warning that AI is about to erase small business websites, take a breath. The reality is narrower, and more manageable, than the panic suggests. Being found across both Google and AI is the whole point of our SEO & AI visibility work.

What an AI Overview actually is

Google's own explanation is that these features rely on retrieval-augmented generation, a technique that uses Google's core search ranking system to find genuinely relevant, current web pages, then generates a summary from what those pages actually say, showing clickable links to the sources underneath. The AI is not inventing an answer from nothing. It is summarizing pages that already exist and already rank.

This matters because it means the AI Overview is not a separate system with its own separate rules to game. It sits directly on top of normal search ranking.

Does it reduce your website traffic?

For some searches, yes, some people get their answer directly in the overview and never click through to any site. This is a real, honest shift worth acknowledging rather than downplaying. But it primarily affects simple factual questions with a single clear answer, not the kind of decision-heavy local searches most small businesses depend on. Someone asking "what time zone is the Philippines" may not click anywhere. Someone asking "best wedding photographer in Batangas" still needs to compare options, look at galleries, and read reviews, which an AI summary is not going to fully replace.

What actually gets cited in an AI Overview

Based on how the system works, three things matter:

Your page has to be eligible to rank normally in the first place. A page that does not appear in the regular organic results has essentially no chance of being cited, since the AI is drawing from that same pool of ranked pages.

Your content has to answer the question clearly and directly. A page with a genuinely helpful, well-organized answer to a specific question is easier for the system to summarize accurately and confidently than a page that buries the answer inside a long, unfocused article.

Your page has to already carry some trust and authority. New websites and new pages take time to earn this, the same way they take time to earn a normal top-ten ranking. There is no shortcut that skips this step for AI visibility specifically.

What you do not need to do

This part matters as much as the part above, because a lot of noise online suggests complicated new tactics. Based on Google's own published guidance, you can safely ignore:

  • Creating special "llms.txt" files or AI-specific markup in the hope of ranking better in Google Search. Google Search does not use them.
  • Breaking your content into tiny fragmented chunks to make it easier for AI to read. Google's systems already understand full pages and multiple topics within them.
  • Rewriting your content in some special "AI-friendly" style. Google's systems understand meaning and synonyms without needing an exact keyword or phrasing match.
  • Creating a separate page for every possible way someone might phrase a search. This one matters more than it might seem. Google explicitly treats this as a spam practice, not a visibility strategy.

What genuinely helps

The advice converges on the same thing that has always mattered for good SEO, described a little differently:

  • Answer real questions clearly, with the direct answer appearing early rather than buried
  • Organize content with clear headings a reader can scan
  • Bring something genuinely useful and specific, not a generic rehash of what every other page already says
  • Keep your technical foundation solid: indexed, crawlable, fast, easy to use on a phone

Will customers ask ChatGPT instead of Google?

This is a related but separate question, and it is one worth taking seriously for local businesses specifically, since tools like ChatGPT are increasingly being asked for local recommendations. We cover that specific angle, and what it means for a Filipino small business, in our companion article on showing up in ChatGPT recommendations, since the mechanics differ from Google's own AI Overviews.

The honest expectation to set

A brand-new website, including this one, does not get cited in AI Overviews immediately, no matter how well the content is written. Citation follows ranking, and ranking on a new domain takes time. This is not a launch-week outcome, for us or for you, and anyone promising otherwise is not being straight with you.

What actually matters right now

Rather than chasing AI visibility as its own separate project, the better use of effort is the same fundamentals covered across the rest of this guide: a complete, active Google Business Profile, a steady flow of real reviews, consistent citations, and genuinely useful content answering the questions your customers actually have. Do that well, over time, and both traditional ranking and AI citation follow from the same work.
