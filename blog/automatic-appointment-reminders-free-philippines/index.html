How to Send Automatic Appointment Reminders for a Clinic or Salon, Free

The free way to send automatic appointment reminders is a booking tool or shared calendar that emails the client automatically when you book them and again before the appointment. Truly free options are email-based, since SMS in the Philippines costs a small amount per message through a gateway, and Messenger has policy limits on messaging people outside a 24-hour window. For most small clinics and salons, the realistic setup is a free booking tool for the confirmation, plus a manual Viber or SMS nudge the day before.

No-shows are one of those costs that never appear on any statement. A slot booked and not filled is money that was already yours, sitting there, gone. And chasing everyone by hand the day before is exactly the kind of small repeated task that eats an hour you did not have. Removing that kind of friction is the heart of our AI & automation work.

Why the reminder works

People do not usually miss appointments because they stopped caring. They miss them because they booked two weeks ago and life happened in between. A reminder the day before catches the ones who genuinely forgot, and it gives the ones who cannot make it a chance to tell you, which means you can refill the slot instead of staring at an empty chair.

Option 1: The free booking tool route

This is where most small businesses should start. A free booking tool does two jobs at once: it stops double-bookings, and it sends the confirmation and reminder automatically. The general setup, whichever tool you pick:

  1. Connect your existing calendar so the tool knows when you are actually free
  2. Set your available hours and how long each service takes
  3. Set buffer time between appointments if you need cleanup or travel time
  4. Turn on the automatic confirmation email and the reminder email
  5. Share your booking link in your Messenger auto-reply, your Google Business Profile, and your Facebook page

The reminder here is usually email. That is the honest limitation of the free tier on most tools, and whether it works for you depends entirely on whether your customers actually read email.

The email problem, said plainly

For a lot of Filipino customers, especially outside corporate or professional services, email is not where they live. They live on Messenger and Viber. An email reminder to someone who checks email twice a month is a reminder that did not happen.

So be realistic about your own customer base. If you run a dental clinic serving office workers, email reminders will land fine. If you run a neighborhood salon where bookings come through Messenger, email reminders will mostly be ignored, and you need a different plan for the day-before nudge.

Option 2: The Google Calendar route

If you do not want a separate booking tool, Google Calendar can carry some of this. When you create an event and add the client as a guest with their email address, Google can send them a notification and reminders based on the event settings. This works, and it is free, but it has the same email dependency, and it requires you to have their email address, which for a walk-in-heavy business you often do not.

Option 3: SMS, and what it actually costs

SMS remains the most reliable way to reach a Filipino customer who is not an email person, because it does not depend on data, an app, or a platform's messaging rules. The honest catch: SMS is not free. Sending automated SMS through a gateway or bulk provider costs a small amount per message. For a business doing twenty appointments a week, that cost is usually trivial against the value of one recovered no-show. For a business doing two hundred, it is worth pricing properly. Check the current rate from an actual provider before you build around it.

Option 4: Viber and Messenger reminders, and the rules that apply

This is where most guides get careless, so here is the accurate version.

Messenger enforces a 24-hour messaging window. You can message freely within 24 hours of the customer's last message to you. Automated messages outside that window are restricted under Meta's policy, with narrow exceptions through approved message tags for certain non-promotional cases. A reminder for an appointment booked three weeks ago falls outside the ordinary window, so you cannot simply automate it through Messenger the way you might expect. Our guide to Messenger and Viber auto-replies covers that window in more detail.

Viber automation through a chatbot carries a monthly cost that is usually hard to justify at small volume.

The practical version: send the day-before Viber message manually, from a saved template, as part of a fixed daily routine. It takes a few minutes for a day's worth of appointments, and it reaches people where they actually are.

The setup we would actually recommend for a small clinic or salon

Layered, not all-or-nothing:

  1. Free booking tool handles the booking itself and the instant confirmation. This stops double-bookings and gives the customer something in writing immediately.
  2. A fixed 15-minute slot in your day, same time every day, where you send day-before reminders manually via Viber or Messenger from a saved template.
  3. Consider paid SMS once the volume makes the manual step painful, or once you can see clearly what no-shows are costing you.

That is not a fully automated system. It is an honest one, and it works with the tools that actually exist rather than the ones a tutorial wishes existed.

How far ahead to send the reminder

The day before is the standard, sent in the afternoon or early evening, when someone can still rearrange their next day. A same-day morning reminder for an afternoon appointment also works well. Two reminders for a routine appointment starts to feel like nagging, so pick one unless the appointment is high-value or far out.

What to put in the reminder

  • What the appointment is for
  • The date and time
  • Where to go
  • A clear, easy way to reschedule, because the reschedule is a win, not a failure

That last one matters more than people think. A customer who reschedules is still a customer. A customer who feels awkward about cancelling just does not show up.
