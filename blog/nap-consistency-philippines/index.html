Getting Your Business Name, Address, and Phone Consistent Across the Web

NAP consistency means your business name, address, and phone number appear exactly the same way on every website, directory, and platform that lists you, including your own website and your Google Business Profile. Even small mismatches, like "St." on one listing and "Street" on another, or an old phone number still showing on one directory from years ago, can quietly confuse Google about whether these are all really the same business, which directly weakens your local search visibility.

This is one of the least exciting things on this list, and one of the most consistently underestimated. Nobody gets excited about checking directory listings. But this quiet, boring consistency work is genuinely part of what separates a business that shows up reliably from one that does not. It's a foundational piece of our SEO & AI visibility work.

Why this actually affects your ranking

Google's local ranking systems partly assess "prominence," how established and trustworthy a business appears, using signals gathered from across the web, not just from your own website. Consistent NAP information across many sources acts like corroborating evidence that you are a real, stable, legitimate business at a specific location. Inconsistent information does the opposite: it creates small doubts that add up, making Google less confident about surfacing you prominently. It's one of the core levers in ranking in the local pack.

Step 1: Decide on your one, official version

Before checking anything, write down the exact, single version of your business name, address, and phone number that you will use everywhere from now on. Small decisions matter here:

  • Full "Street" or abbreviated "St."?
  • Your legal business name, or a shortened version customers actually call you?
  • Landline, mobile, or both listed, and in what order?

Pick one version and treat it as the master copy. Every other listing gets matched to this, not the other way around.

Step 2: Check your own website and Google Business Profile first

Before worrying about outside directories, confirm your own website's contact page and your Google Business Profile match each other exactly. This is the foundation everything else gets compared against, and it is surprisingly common for these two to already disagree, especially if the website was built by one person and the Google listing set up by someone else, months apart. If your profile isn't set up yet, start with our guide to setting up your Google Business Profile.

Step 3: Search for existing listings you may not know about

Search your business name plus your city, and separately your phone number on its own, in quotes. This often surfaces old directory listings you never created yourself, sometimes years old, sometimes from a previous address or an old number. These orphaned listings are common and worth finding deliberately rather than hoping they do not matter.

Step 4: Claim and correct what you find

For any listing you find with outdated or incorrect information, most directories allow you to claim the listing as the owner and correct it directly. This step takes real time, since each directory has its own claiming process, but it is one of the more direct ways to clean up inconsistency that is actively working against you.

Step 5: Get listed on the directories that matter for the Philippines

Beyond correcting what already exists, deliberately building a presence on the directories most relevant to Philippine local search adds legitimate citation strength. A reasonable starting list, prioritizing the higher-authority ones first, includes Yelp Philippines, Yellow Pages PH, and other established Philippine-specific business directories.

Focus on quality over quantity here. A handful of accurate, well-maintained listings on directories people and search engines actually trust does more for you than dozens of listings on directories nobody has heard of.

Step 6: Recheck after any business change

Any time your business moves location, changes its phone number, or rebrands its name, the same inconsistency risk reappears immediately, across every listing you have ever created. This is the moment most businesses forget to update anything beyond their own website and Google profile, leaving old, wrong information sitting on directories for years. Building a habit of updating every known listing whenever something changes saves a much bigger cleanup project later.

A simple way to track this without a paid tool

A basic spreadsheet works fine: one row per directory, columns for the listing URL, the current status of your information there, and the date you last checked it. This turns an invisible, easy-to-forget task into something you can actually see progress on, and revisit quarterly rather than never.

What this looks like done properly, versus done halfway

Done halfway looks like: Google profile correct, website correct, and then silence, with old directory listings quietly sitting wrong indefinitely. Done properly looks like: one master version of your information, actively checked and corrected across your most relevant directories, revisited any time something about the business changes. The second version is not dramatically more work. It is the same work, just finished instead of started. Our one-weekend local SEO checklist folds this into a routine you can actually keep.
